# What percentage of leads really come from ChatGPT? Analytics says under 2%. Companies that ask say 7% to 25%

By **Stanislav Peev**, SEO consultant & GEO specialist · Published **September 21, 2026** · 18 sources checked against the original · 10 min read

<2% vs 7-25%

What percentage of leads come from ChatGPT and other AI assistants? Every large analytics dataset puts them under 2% of tracked traffic and leads, and as low as 0.073% of inbound calls. Companies that ask new customers, or publish their own signup data, report 7% to 25%. Both are measured correctly. They measure different things.

Tracked layer confirmed by 3 independent datasets: CallRail (about 20M leads), Octane11 (25M sessions), Ahrefs (3,000 sites). Disclosed layer: Outfunnel, Webflow, Vercel, Tally.

## Key takeaways

- **0.073%** - the share of inbound calls to small and mid-sized businesses that CallRail could trace to AI search across about 20 million leads. Legal was highest at 0.175% (CallRail, January 2026)
- **Under 2%** - AI search as a share of 25 million B2B search sessions, September 2025 to March 2026 (Octane11). The average website got 0.17% of its traffic from AI chatbots in early 2025 (Ahrefs)
- **0.75% to 7.29%** - leads naming "ChatGPT / AI" in Outfunnel's "How did you hear about us?" field, first half of 2024 against first half of 2025. The only published self-reported series I could find
- **8%, 10%, 25%** - share of new signups from AI disclosed by Webflow (June 2025), Vercel (April 2025) and Tally (spring 2025). All three sell to people who live in ChatGPT, so read them as the ceiling
- **55.9%** - of the visits that follow a ChatGPT recommendation arrive through a branded search, not a click from the chat (Similarweb panel). That is where most of the gap goes
- **45% to 94%** - buyers who say they use AI to research: 45% of US consumers for local businesses (BrightLocal), 51% of B2B software buyers start there (G2), 94% of business buyers use it somewhere in the process (Forrester)
- **4 popular figures** did not survive a check against their stated source, including the "up to 30% of leads" number that circulates in agency posts. Listed below

## 1. The short answer

It depends on who is counting. Analytics and call tracking count a lead as AI-sourced only when the visitor clicked a link inside the chat, and on that definition the answer is under 2% everywhere and under 0.2% for local service businesses. Ask the customer instead and the answer is several times higher, because most people read the recommendation, close the chat and search for the brand by name.

I started pulling these numbers together after an r/SEO thread in September 2026 where a B2B marketer asked whether anyone had traced a real lead back to ChatGPT or Perplexity. He was explicit that he did not want mention counts or visibility scores. Twenty replies later he had a handful of anecdotes, one of them worth "nearly $10k MRR", and no figure he could put in front of a CFO. The week before, a small-business owner asking the same question on r/smallbusiness collected 82 comments and the same result.

The figures exist. They are scattered across vendor reports, founder posts and consumer surveys, and the statistics roundups that collect them drop all of them into one flat list, so "94% of buyers use AI" sits next to "0.17% of traffic" with no explanation of why both can be true. Below they are sorted into three layers, each checked against the original publication.

## 2. How many buyers say they use AI to research?

Between 45% and 94%, depending on the audience and how loosely "use" is defined. These are the numbers that open most conference talks, and they are real. They describe behaviour before a purchase. None of them says a lead arrived.

| Survey | Figure | Who was asked | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Used an AI tool for local business recommendations | 45% | 1,002 US adults, up from 6% a year earlier | BrightLocal, March 2026 |
| Start vendor research in an AI chatbot | 51% | B2B software buyers | G2 Buyer Behavior Report 2026 |
| Use AI somewhere in the buying process | 94% | Business buyers, 89% a year earlier | Forrester, January 2026 |

The BrightLocal jump from 6% to 45% in a single year is the one I would show a local business owner, because it comes with a second number. Of the 455 respondents who had used AI for a recommendation, 88% said they go and check the sources behind it. They look the business up. For a plumber or a dental clinic that check usually ends on a Google Business Profile, which is why [the state of your reviews](https://stanislav-peev.com/research/google-review-concentration-home-services/) still decides whether the AI recommendation turns into a call.

Two cautions before anyone pastes 25% into a pitch deck. These are self-serve software products whose buyers are developers, designers and marketers, the heaviest ChatGPT users there are. And companies publish a figure like this when it flatters them, so the sample is selected for good news. I read the range as a ceiling for a business with a technical audience. A roofing company should not expect it.

Outfunnel's table has a detail that gets less attention than the headline. Google search fell only from 19.57% to 17.25% over the same year, and "can't remember" dropped from 8.5% to 4.2%. AI did not replace search in their funnel. It took some of the answers people used to be vague about.

Cognism's 2026 inbound report points the same way from the other side: LLMs grew 345% year over year as a last-touch source for its MQLs, organic traffic fell 33.6%, and direct MQLs rose 6%. People research somewhere the site never sees and come back by typing the name.

## 4. What does analytics actually record?

Almost nothing. CallRail attributes 0.073% of inbound calls to AI search across about 20 million leads for small and mid-sized businesses. Octane11 puts AI search under 2% of 25 million B2B search sessions. Ahrefs found the average site gets 0.17% of its traffic from AI chatbots.

0.073%

of inbound calls to small and mid-sized businesses originate from AI search. By industry: legal 0.175%, manufacturing 0.093%, marketing agencies 0.091%. ChatGPT accounts for 90.1% of those calls, Perplexity 6.3%, Gemini 2.4%, Claude 1.2%.

Source: CallRail, analysis of about 20M inbound leads, January 27, 2026

That is roughly seven calls in every ten thousand. If you run a local service business and someone shows you a dashboard with AI referrals as a line item, this is the scale to expect on it today.

The B2B funnel data is thin in a different way. HockeyStack followed LLM sessions through 118 accounts, and most of them had not closed a single deal they could trace to an LLM session.

## 5. Why is the tracked number 10 to 100 times smaller?

Because most people do not click the link in the chat. Similarweb followed real users in a US desktop panel after ChatGPT recommended a brand: they were 2.5 times more likely to visit that brand's site within seven days, and 55.9% of those visits came through a branded search. Analytics files every one of them under organic or paid brand.

### Where the visit shows up after ChatGPT recommends a brand

More than half of the follow-up visits carry no trace of the assistant that caused them.

*Source: Similarweb, US desktop panel, July 2025 to January 2026; finance, travel and beauty.*

The visitors who do arrive that way are not casual. In the same panel they viewed 12.0 pages over 11.8 minutes, against 6.5 pages and 5.6 minutes for everyone else.

There are three ways an AI-created lead loses its label, and Radyant's attribution guide names them well. A copied URL or a click from the mobile app carries no referrer and lands as Direct. A buyer who reads the answer and then searches the brand lands as Organic or Paid Brand. And a recommendation with no link at all produces no event of any kind until the person shows up days later from somewhere else.

The BrightLocal finding from section 2 belongs here too. If 88% of AI users check the source before acting, then a second touch is the normal path and last-click will credit that second touch. What they find when they check matters as well. I collected the published figures on how often assistants get basic business facts wrong in [a separate report](https://stanislav-peev.com/research/ai-wrong-business-information-statistics/), and a wrong phone number ends the journey before any attribution model gets a say.

Dreamdata's own instance shows the same leak from inside one company: LLM-surfaced content influenced 1.1% of its MQLs over twelve months and 6.2% of new business closed. The influence shows up late in the deal record, long after the session that would have carried the referrer.

## 7. Estimate your own attribution gap

Two ways to size what your analytics is missing. The first uses your own form data if you already ask new leads how they found you. The second applies Similarweb's visit split, which is cruder: it assumes your AI-influenced visitors behave like that panel, where only 44.1% arrived by a route other than branded search. Treat the result as a rough floor and replace it with your own form data as soon as you have a month of it.

Benchmarks used: tracked share under 2% (CallRail, Octane11, Ahrefs); asked or disclosed share 7.3% to 25% (Outfunnel, Webflow, Vercel, Tally); 55.9% of post-recommendation visits arrive via branded search (Similarweb). Nothing you type leaves this page.

## 8. Four numbers I could not trace

While checking each figure against the publication it is credited to, four of the most repeated ones did not hold as credited. I have left them out of the table and list them here so nobody has to repeat the search.

- **"Up to 30% of leads self-report AI discovery" (credited to Radyant's 2026 client data).** It appears in several agency posts with that credit. Radyant's own attribution guide, as I read it on September 21, 2026, does not contain it. The guide uses "20% of your closed-won deals" as a hypothetical example. If there is a primary publication I will add it here.
- **"Forrester: 94% of buyers use AI" linked to the press release.** The figure is real, but the 94% in Forrester's press release of January 21, 2026 is about buyers in groups of six or more reporting benefits from the group. The AI-use 94% is in an analyst blog post published a day later. Many roundups link the wrong page.
- **"CallRail: AI search converts at 14.2% against 2.8% for Google."** The figure is real but belongs to someone else. It comes from Opollo's B2B tech data, which I cite in [my conversion report](https://stanislav-peev.com/research/ai-referral-traffic-conversion-vs-organic/). It is not in CallRail's press release, its marketing VP's published Q&A, or the Search Engine Journal write-up, yet at least one agency post credits it to the CallRail study.
- **A weekly figure for Tally of 44% of signups from AI assistants.** I found it only in search snippets. The 25% in the table comes from trade coverage quoting the co-founder directly.

## 9. What to do on Monday

Add one free-text question to every form and every first call: "How did you first hear about us?" It costs nothing and is the only signal that survives a closed tab. Outfunnel's series exists because they have asked it for years.

- **Make it free text, not a dropdown.** People write "chat", "ChatGpt", "asked AI" and "Perplexity then Google". A dropdown with no AI option is how the answer ends up under "Other".
- **Ask about the first touch.** "How did you *first* hear" and "what made you contact us today" get different answers, and you want the first.
- **Read three signals side by side:** the form field, AI referral sessions in analytics, and branded search impressions in Search Console. A rise in the first and third with a flat second is the pattern the Similarweb data predicts.
- **Treat visibility scores as the leading indicator only.** They move before leads do, and they are noisy. In [my 60-query reliability test](https://stanislav-peev.com/research/ai-visibility-tracking-reliability/), the same prompt gave a different top brand in about one run out of five.
- **Work on being the recommended name, since the click is rare.** Which pages get cited depends on how the buyer phrases the question. I measured that split in [AI citations by question shape](https://stanislav-peev.com/research/ai-citations-by-query-shape/).

For a local business the realistic reading is this. Tracked AI leads will stay a rounding error for a while, a growing share of the people calling you consulted an assistant first, and they verified you on Google before dialling. So the work still sits in the things they verify, which is what [small business SEO](https://stanislav-peev.com/services/small-business-seo/) has always covered. If you want to know whether assistants can name you at all, that is one of the checks in an [SEO audit](https://stanislav-peev.com/services/seo-audit/). The rest of my [research](https://stanislav-peev.com/research/) is on the same theme, and how I work with clients is on the [services page](https://stanislav-peev.com/services/).

## 10. Methodology

I looked for every published figure that answers some version of "what share of leads, signups, calls or sessions comes from AI assistants", plus the survey figures usually quoted next to them. Searches ran on September 21, 2026 across Google, an academic index (OpenAlex, which returned nothing relevant - this question lives in industry data) and Perplexity, to see which sources assistants already cite for it. I read 31 pages and kept 18 sources.

Each figure was checked against the full text of the page it is credited to, not against the article quoting it. A figure is marked *verified* when the original states it with its method, *cross-verified* when the original was not retrievable and two independent reports agree (G2, Similarweb), and *single-source* when it rests on one disclosure (the Vercel and Tally founder posts, Dreamdata's worked example, Demandbase through trade coverage). Of 19 facts, 10 are verified, 4 cross-verified and 5 single-source. Twelve were published in 2026 and seven in 2025. Figures that could not be found at their credited source were dropped and are listed in section 8.

## 11. Limitations

The three layers measure different populations as well as different things, so the distance between them is not purely attribution loss. Part of it is that Tally's customers are not CallRail's customers.

Four more. Every source in the tracked layer sells attribution, call tracking or SEO software, and there is no government or academic dataset on this question. The disclosed layer is five software companies that chose to publish, so it is biased toward good news and toward technical audiences. Outfunnel does not state its sample size. And the figures span February 2025 to June 2026 in a channel where Demandbase saw monthly volume double in a single month, so the older ones are already low. I will re-check every row quarterly and date the changes.

## 12. Frequently asked questions

**What percentage of leads come from ChatGPT?**

It depends on how you count. Tracked by analytics, under 2%: CallRail attributes 0.073% of about 20 million inbound calls to AI search, and Octane11 puts AI search under 2% of 25 million B2B sessions. Asked directly, companies report far more: 7.29% of Outfunnel's leads named ChatGPT or AI in the first half of 2025, and Webflow, Vercel and Tally disclosed 8%, 10% and 25% of new signups.

**Why does GA4 show almost no AI traffic when customers say they found us on ChatGPT?**

Because most people do not click the link in the chat. In Similarweb's US panel, 55.9% of the visits that followed a ChatGPT recommendation arrived through a branded search, which analytics files under organic or paid brand. Copied URLs and mobile app clicks land as Direct. BrightLocal found 88% of AI users check the source before acting, so a second touch is the normal path.

**Which AI assistant sends the most leads?**

ChatGPT, by a wide margin, though its share is falling. It accounted for 90.1% of AI-sourced calls in CallRail's January 2026 data, with Perplexity at 6.3%, Gemini 2.4% and Claude 1.2%. In Octane11's B2B sessions ChatGPT's share of AI referrals fell from 86% in October 2025 to 72% in February 2026, while Gemini grew from 4.5% to 13.0%.

**How many local customers use AI to pick a business?**

45% of US consumers said they had used an AI tool for local business recommendations in BrightLocal's 2026 survey of 1,002 adults, up from 6% a year earlier. Tracked calls tell a smaller story: 0.073% of inbound calls in CallRail's data, highest in legal at 0.175%. The difference is mostly people who asked an assistant and then looked the business up on Google.

**Do AI-referred leads close?**

Some do, most accounts cannot show it yet. Across 118 B2B accounts HockeyStack found a median 5.24% of LLM visits became hand-raisers and 2.66% of those reached qualified pipeline, but the median for pipeline to closed-won was 0%. Dreamdata's own data shows LLM-surfaced content influencing 1.1% of MQLs and 6.2% of new business closed over twelve months.

**How should I measure AI-sourced leads?**

Ask. Add a free-text "How did you first hear about us?" to every form and first call, then read it next to AI referral sessions and branded search impressions. Outfunnel's figure of 0.75% rising to 7.29% comes from exactly that field. Cognism saw LLMs grow 345% as a last-touch MQL source while direct MQLs rose 6% and organic traffic fell 33.6%.
